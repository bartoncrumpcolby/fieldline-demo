It’s all in the book

No implementation project, no training day. The spreadsheet the home already keeps is the way in, and the system takes over the remembering.

STEP ONE

Paste the sheet

Copy the training matrix out of Excel. Staffbook reads names, courses and dates, shows what it understood, and asks about anything it is not sure of. Three minutes.

STEP TWO

The matrix comes alive

Every certificate gets its renewal worked out. Expired is red today, due-soon turns amber ahead of time, and the dashboard names who to chase first.

STEP THREE

Monday reports in

One plain email before the week starts: expired, due soon, who has not signed the new policy. A quiet week sends nothing at all.

No child records. That is the point.

There is no field for a young person anywhere in Staffbook, so trying it never puts a child’s data anywhere new. It sits beside whatever care system the home already runs, holding the staff side: training, DBS, safer recruitment, supervisions, policies signed "read and understood", the home’s checks, and the one-click evidence pack.

Built alongside a serving Responsible Individual with twenty years in children’s residential care. The screens answer what inspections actually ask, because she kept asking.

Straight answers

We already use a care system. Why this too?

Keep it. Care systems hold the young people’s records and treat the staff side as a bolt-on. Staffbook only does the staff side, so it goes deeper, and it sits beside your system rather than replacing it.

Who is behind this?

One person in Manchester, building it with a Responsible Individual who has run homes for twenty years. You talk to the person who built it.

What if you disappear?

Your data is never hostage. Everything exports to plain spreadsheets whenever you like. Worst case, you are back to Excel with better-organised files.

Is the data safe?

UK-hosted, every organisation walled off from every other at the database level, private files, append-only audit trail. The long version is written for your DPO.
