Where it deliberately stops

The boundaries are structural, not settings someone can change later.

No child records

There is no child record in Staffbook and no field intended for one. It is a staff and premises system, and it stays one.

No DBS content

Certificate number, dates and status. There is nowhere to type what a disclosure says, on purpose.

Pay stays private

A support worker sees their own record and the rota. Wages are visible to the Responsible Individual and nobody else, enforced in the database rather than promised in a policy.

The detailed version of these boundaries, and how the system that holds your staff records is itself protected, has its own page: the security page. Written for the person who asks how it is built before they ask what it costs.

Why it only does the staff side

Not a cut-down anything. The scope is the design: when an inspection goes wrong on paperwork, it is almost always the staff file, and this is the tool built to make that impossible.

One job, done completely

Staff compliance here is the whole product, engineered like it: receipts on every task, sign offs item by item, an audit trail nobody can edit, an evidence pack in one click. A system that does everything gives this job a module. Staffbook gives it everything.

No child data, by design

There is no field for a young person anywhere in it. That is not a missing feature, it is the security model: the most sensitive records in your organisation never enter a new system because of Staffbook, and nobody has to sign that risk off.

Working by Friday

No migration, no retraining, no months of settling in. The manager uploads staff files and reads the board the same day, and nothing about how the home cares for children changes. Software should cost a home an afternoon, not a quarter.

Easy to leave, on purpose

Monthly rolling, leave any month, your data exported to you and deleted. A tool this sure of its own usefulness does not need a 36 month contract to keep you, and the exit being clean is exactly why trying it costs nothing.

It sits beside whatever else you run, paper daily logs or a full care records platform, and owns the one file inspections actually turn on: the staff and premises record, in date, provable.

Reasonable questions

Who can see what?

Four roles. A support worker sees their own file and the rota. A senior adds the team’s checks. A manager runs training, supervisions and the rota, but never pay or restricted records. The Responsible Individual sees everything. The database enforces this, not the screens, so it holds even if the software has a bug.

Where does the data live?

UK GDPR applies in full. The providers are named openly on the security page, and their regions are fixed in the processor agreement you sign before any real record is entered, so where your data sits is on paper, not on trust.

What happens when staff record their own training?

It waits. Nothing a support worker records counts towards compliance until a manager confirms it, because it is your name against the record at inspection.

How does leaving work?

One email. Everything the system holds for your organisation is exported to you, then deleted within 30 days, subject only to records you are required to retain, which leave with you first.
